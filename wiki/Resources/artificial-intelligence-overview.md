# Artificial Intelligence Overview

## Artificial Intelligence (AI)

**Definition**: AI is the capability of computational systems to perform tasks typically associated with human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, and decision-making. It is a field of research spanning computer science, engineering, and mathematics.

### Key Applications
- Advanced web search engines
- Chatbots and virtual assistants
- Autonomous vehicles
- Strategy game analysis (e.g., chess, Go)
- Content generation (text, images, audio, video)

### Historical Context
- **1956**: Founded as an academic discipline.
- **AI Winters**: Periods of disappointment and loss of funding alternating with optimism.
- **2012**: Funding increased due to GPU acceleration of neural networks and the rise of deep learning.
- **2017**: Growth accelerated with the transformer architecture.
- **2020s**: AI boom spurred by generative AI advances.

### Core Research Goals

#### 1. Reasoning and Problem-Solving
- Early algorithms imitated step-by-step logical deduction.
- Modern **Reasoning Models** (LLMs trained on chains of thought) emerged in 2024, improving performance in math and coding but prone to hallucinations.
- Challenge: Combinatorial explosion in large problems.

#### 2. Knowledge Representation
- **Symbolic Approach**: Uses formal logic and ontologies; struggles with commonsense knowledge breadth and acquisition.
- **Machine Learning Approach**: Acquires knowledge from large datasets (books, websites) without explicit symbolic representation; better at generalization but can struggle with accurate recall.

#### 3. Planning and Decision-Making
- **Agents**: Entities that perceive and act to achieve goals.
- **Rational Agents**: Maximize expected utility based on preferences and probabilities.
- **Markov Decision Processes (MDP)**: Model transitions and rewards to determine optimal policies.
- **Game Theory**: Used for multi-agent interactions.

#### 4. Learning
- **Unsupervised Learning**: Finds patterns in unlabeled data.
- **Supervised Learning**: Uses labeled data for classification or regression.
- **Reinforcement Learning**: Learns via rewards and punishments.
- **Transfer Learning**: Applies knowledge from one problem to another.
- **Deep Learning**: Uses artificial neural networks for all the above types.

#### 5. Natural Language Processing (NLP)
- Enables reading, writing, and communication in human languages.
- **Key Techniques**: Word embeddings, Transformers (attention mechanisms), and Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPT).
- **Milestones**: By 2023, models achieved human-level scores on exams like the Bar Exam and SAT.

#### 6. Perception
- Uses sensors (cameras, microphones, lidar) to deduce aspects of the world.
- Includes computer vision, speech recognition, and object tracking.

#### 7. Social Intelligence
- **Affective Computing**: Systems that recognize and simulate human emotions.
- Applications include sentiment analysis and conversational agents.

### Techniques

#### Search and Optimization
- **State Space Search**: Searches through trees of possible states (e.g., planning, adversarial search for games).
- **Local Search**: Uses mathematical optimization (e.g., gradient descent for neural networks, evolutionary computation).

#### Logic
- **Propositional Logic**: Operates on true/false statements.
- **Predicate Logic**: Operates on objects, predicates, and relations with quantifiers.
