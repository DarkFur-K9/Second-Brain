# Vannevar Bush's Memex Concept

# Memex

A **memex** (a portmanteau of "memory" and "index") is a hypothetical electromechanical device for interacting with microform documents, described in Vannevar Bush's 1945 article *"As We May Think"*.

## Core Concept

Bush envisioned the memex as a device in which individuals would compress and store all of their books, records, and communications, "mechanized so that it may be consulted with exceeding speed and flexibility." It was intended to serve as an automatic personal filing system, acting as "an enlarged intimate supplement to his memory."

The concept influenced the development of early hypertext systems and personal knowledge base software. While Bush's hypothetical implementation relied on static microfilm pages and lacked a true hypertext system with internal page structure, it proposed storing information analogous to the mental association of the human brain.

## Key Features

- **Associative Trails**: A way to create a new linear sequence of microfilm frames across arbitrary sequences by creating chained links, along with personal comments and side trails.
- **Input Mechanisms**: Users could record new information via photos from paper or touch-sensitive screens, and insert comments linked into main or side trails.
- **Sharing**: Users could copy interesting trails (containing references and annotations) and pass them to friends for insertion into their own memex.
- **Storage**: Based on microfilm reels, dry photography, and analog computing, allowing for a huge, indexed repository of knowledge.

## Legacy and Influence

- **Douglas Engelbart**: Inspired by Bush's proposal for co-evolution between humans and machines, Engelbart developed technical designs leading to the personal computer, electronic visual display, and mouse.
- **Ted Nelson**: Coined the word "hypertext" in 1965, quoting Bush's memex idea at length.
- **Tim Berners-Lee**: Built ENQUIRE software at CERN in 1980, which later led to the invention of the World Wide Web in 1989, confirming the legitimacy of his endeavor through the work of predecessors like Bush, Engelbart, and Nelson.
- **DARPA Memex Program**: In 2014, DARPA launched a program using the name Memex to fight human trafficking on the dark web, citing Bush's device as inspiration. The resulting AI search technologies were released as open-source software.

## Evolution of the Concept

- **1959 (Memex II)**: Bush described an improved version where professional societies would maintain 'master memex' systems with interconnected papers, references, and tables.
- **1967 (Memex Revisited)**: In *Science Is Not Enough*, Bush detailed technological advancements (photocells, transistors, magnetic tape) that made his vision possible. He emphasized that the goal was not swift access, but **selective access**, noting that indexing systems still relied too much on linear pathways rather than association-based systems.
